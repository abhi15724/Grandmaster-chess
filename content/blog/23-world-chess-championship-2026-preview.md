Chess's biggest event of the year is set: the reigning World Champion, India's **D Gukesh**, will defend his title against Uzbekistan's **Javokhir Sindarov** in Geneva, Switzerland, from **November 25 to December 15, 2026**. It's already being called the youngest World Championship match in the sport's history — both players turn 20 within weeks of the first game.

*This page reflects what's confirmed as of late August 2026. A few logistical details (exact venue, ticketing, and broadcast partners) hadn't been announced yet as of this writing — check FIDE's official site closer to the match for those specifics.*

## Dates and venue

FIDE confirmed the match on July 28, 2026: Geneva was chosen as a neutral host city, over bids that reportedly included Cyprus. The match runs **November 25 – December 15, 2026**.

## Format: how the title actually gets decided

- **14 classical games.** The first player to reach **7½ points** wins outright — no need to play out all 14 if someone clinches it earlier.
- **If it's tied 7–7 after all 14 games**, the match moves to **rapid tiebreaks**, and then **blitz tiebreaks** if rapid is also tied — the same escalating format FIDE has used in recent championship cycles.

## How the two players got here

**Gukesh** doesn't need to qualify — he's the defending champion, having won the title in December 2024 by defeating China's Ding Liren in the 14th and final game of their match in Singapore, becoming the youngest World Champion in history at 18 years old.

**Sindarov** earned his shot the hard way: after winning the 2025 FIDE World Cup in Goa, India (becoming the youngest World Cup champion ever in the process), he then won the 2026 FIDE Candidates Tournament in Cyprus with a dominant, undefeated 10/14 score, clinching first place with a full round to spare — finishing ahead of a field that included Fabiano Caruana, Hikaru Nakamura, and Anish Giri. He's the first Uzbek player in history to reach a World Championship match. [Read more about who Sindarov is](/blog/who-is-javokhir-sindarov) if you're not familiar with him yet.

## The storyline: form favors the challenger

Here's what makes this matchup genuinely uncertain rather than a coronation: recent form clearly favors Sindarov. He enters the match near his career-peak rating, ranked around World No. 4, and has been widely described as the form player of the year. Gukesh, by contrast, had a difficult stretch through mid-2026 — including a last-place finish at Norway Chess in June and a further rating drop after the Chennai Grand Masters — dropping him outside the world's top 25 for a period, a striking fall for a reigning World Champion. None of that erases what Gukesh achieved to win the title in the first place, but it does mean this match is a genuine contest between a champion trying to rediscover his best form and a red-hot challenger arriving at the peak of his.

## How to follow the match

Official broadcast and ticketing details are typically finalized by FIDE in the weeks leading up to a Championship match — check **fide.com** directly closer to November for confirmed streaming details, ticket information, and the exact playing venue within Geneva. Major chess sites (Chess.com, ChessBase, World Chess) traditionally provide live move-by-move coverage, commentary, and analysis alongside the official broadcast, so any of those are safe bets for following along once the match begins.
